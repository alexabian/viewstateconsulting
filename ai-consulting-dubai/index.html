AI consulting and adoption support in Dubai

Practical AI adoption for Dubai companies: readiness review, rolling out Claude or ChatGPT with admin and data rules, workflow automation, and private self-hosted AI.

Quick answer·Reviewed 27 October 2026

Viewstate helps Dubai companies put AI tools to work without losing control of company data.

The work is deliberately practical: a readiness review of where AI helps and what it risks, a rollout of tools such as Claude or ChatGPT with admin, data rules and training, automation of repetitive document and reporting work, and private AI for data that cannot leave the building.

Engagements are a pilot project or an ongoing advisory cadence, sized for companies without an AI team.

01 / Scope

What this covers

AI readiness review
Where AI genuinely helps in this business, what it risks, and what a first pilot should be. Written so a non-technical director can decide.
Rollout and governance
Claude or ChatGPT deployed with the right plans, admin controls, data rules and training, so staff use it consistently instead of improvising.
Workflow automation
Repetitive document, email and reporting work automated with appropriate review points, measured against time saved.
Private and self-hosted AI
Local models for data that cannot leave the building, including the hardware and operational trade-offs involved.
Data protection and policy
A usable internal policy on what may be pasted into which tool, written by someone who has to enforce it.
02 / Fit

Who this is for

  • A company where staff already use ChatGPT on personal accounts and nobody knows what is being pasted in.
  • A business that wants a first AI pilot with a measurable outcome rather than a workshop.
  • An organisation with client data or regulated information that cannot go to a public service.
  • A leadership team that needs a defensible position on AI before a client or auditor asks.

05 / Questions

Common questions

Where should a company start with AI?
With one workflow that is repetitive, text-heavy and clearly measurable, run as a short pilot with named owners. Readiness comes before tools: the first question is what data may be used, not which product to buy.
How do you keep company data out of public AI tools?
By combining policy with configuration: appropriate plans and admin controls, clear rules on what may be entered where, and, where the data cannot leave the building, private models running on your own infrastructure.
Do you build custom AI applications?
The focus is adoption and operations rather than software development: choosing, deploying, governing and automating with existing tools, plus private model hosting where that is the requirement.
Is private or self-hosted AI realistic for a small company?
It is increasingly realistic, and the trade-offs are honest ones: hardware cost and operational effort against data staying entirely in-house. For a small company it usually makes sense for one specific dataset rather than everything.
